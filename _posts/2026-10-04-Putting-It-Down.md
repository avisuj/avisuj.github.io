My dad called to say something was wrong with my daughter. I told him I didn't know what to do and asked him to call the school.

I've replayed that call for years. I was a father hearing something frightening, and I handed it to someone else. There were two more moments like it. Someone asked me a hard question, and someone told me something I couldn't hold. Both times I shut down.

My daughter was raped. I'm not going to tell her story here, because it's hers. I'm going to tell mine, which is what it has been like to be her father since then.

I've been trying to put all of it down at once, and I can't. It's a pile of separate things. My counselor and my wife have helped me see that, and I can set them down one at a time.

The first is the verdict. For years the sentence in my head was "I failed her." It feels like honesty. My wife pushed on it. She said putting this down doesn't mean pretending I didn't fall short in a moment. It means remembering that I'm not defined by my lowest one. Peter denied Jesus, and on the shore Jesus asked him if he loved him and then gave him sheep to feed (John 21:15-17). The denial was named, and it didn't become his name.

The second is a story I told myself. If I weren't so insecure from how I grew up, I would have stayed curious and been present. My counselor challenged that. Most parents are insecure. Most parents freeze when someone tells them the worst thing they can imagine. The Catechism says fear and ignorance can lessen responsibility (CCC 1735). I'd been treating a reflex as a choice.

The third is the belief that a careful enough father would have prevented it. I don't think it's true, but I keep reaching for it. If it's true, the world still makes sense. I'd rather blame myself than admit this can happen to a child whose father loves her.

The fourth is my anger at my parents. I told them about it recently without yelling. That's all I have on that one. I'm not ready to put it down, and I don't think I'm supposed to rush.

The fifth is the feeling that joy would be disloyal. I've lost a lot of it this year, even at small wins. Hebrews says Jesus endured the cross for the joy set before him, despising its shame (12:2). He didn't owe the shame anything. I'm trying to learn I don't either.

There's a lot left. My wife and I grieve mostly apart. I talk to her about work because it's what I can say without breaking down.

But some things have changed. We moved to St. Louis. I stopped forcing people to perform, and I leave the shielding to God. My wife and I are acting like we're on the same team. I went to the prayer ministry, and I went to Mass. They aren't small.

Lord, I can't carry all of it. Today I'm giving you the first piece, the sentence that says I failed her. I'll see about the next one tomorrow.
