Ok God —

Let me start with the hard part, because I don't want to bury it under anything softer.

Tough conversation with my mom this week. She and Dad have been texting the kids, playing the victim in a story where they're not the ones who did the harm. I felt three things land at once — rage, the need to protect my kids, and underneath both, the quieter wish that they could still have a relationship with their grandparents, if they ever choose to. I didn't handle it well. She didn't remember the next day. Maybe. That should feel like mercy. It doesn't.

Here's what I actually learned tonight, and I'm done softening it: I don't forgive them. Not the "don't convert." Not the years of threatened disowning. Not how they treated my marriage. Not watching Abby go through what she went through while they stood by. Not the entitlement that never let up. I know they love me. Knowing that doesn't cancel the debt.

So here's the question underneath the question: how to love them and still call the wound a wound? I'm done trying to figure it out by myself - I need you.

I know you're with me, and I can feel the difference in where I'm standing now. I pulled back from my family of origin because I had to. And on the other side of that pulling back, something got lighter. My family is better in St. Louis. Missy and the kids have more support around them. We're not in crisis anymore — we're working on how we talk to each other, which is its own slow labor. Some days are better than others. But I'm staying in the moment.

That's not nothing. That's evidence that presence is possible even while the deeper wound is still open. Which brings me to the part I think matters most tonight — not what's being done around me, but what's actually changing in me.

I'm grateful my escapes got boring. My real life became more interesting than the ways I used to hide from it. I want to sit with that for a second, because I think it's the whole post in one sentence. For a long time the exit was more compelling than the room. Now it isn't. That's not willpower. That's healing showing up as a change in appetite. I've failed myself and I've failed you plenty along the way — I'm learning to be kinder to myself about that without letting myself off the hook. You didn't come for the people who had it together. Your yoke is easy because I'm not carrying it alone. I'm learning to trust that, even though some part of me still doesn't feel worthy to have you under my roof.

Work is hard right now. But you've put good people in my path, and I don't take that for granted. The work still matters. It can still do real good. I'm learning to manage what I feel when expectations never quite line up — everyone's trying, but the day-to-day is where it gets tangled. That's where I need to be more like Fr. Ciszek: trusting your path even when I can't map it. You make every day an adventure. It keeps reminding me how much I still don't know.

What I don't know is why the feeling I carried tonight — the rage, the "I don't forgive them" — still runs so deep after all these years of treating the surface of it instead of the depth. I don't want to fix that tonight. I want to just see it. Let it be what it is, without rushing to explain it or resolve it or turn it into a lesson before I've even felt it all the way through.

Maybe that's the actual prayer: not help me understand this, but help me sit in it long enough to see what it's actually made of. I don't need every answer tonight. I've never needed every answer — I need to trust that you're not finished, the way Job had to trust it in the middle of everything he couldn't explain. He didn't get the reasons. He got you, in the whirlwind, still at work. That's what I'm asking for too. Not resolution. Just the trust that you haven't stopped.
