Lord,

This week I wrote a list of what I need from my parents to repair our relationship. Six lines. Each one starts with "I need." When I finished, I noticed that nothing on it was something I had done.

I've been reading Janis Abrahms Spring, *How Can I Forgive You?* She describes a few ways people respond to being hurt. Cheap forgiveness skips the harm and says it's fine. Refusing to forgive holds the grudge. Genuine forgiveness takes two people. The one who caused the harm acknowledges it, feels the weight of it, and changes. Acceptance is what's left when they won't, or can't.

I know the first two. For years I did cheap forgiveness. Sunday dinners after a wedding they skipped. A call to my mom every day. I kept showing up and called it love. In July I wrote that I don't forgive them, and that was the grudge, said honestly. Neither one got me anywhere.

Acceptance is where I am now. Spring describes it as work you do alone, and it has parts. For me there are three.

**See the harm clearly.**

At sixteen, my father told me he would stop supporting me if I didn't obey. At eighteen, he did. When I told my mother I wanted to become Catholic, she took me to India for three weeks. My father didn't come to my wedding. He made comments about my wife's body. Something happened to my daughter, and my father has never said that it did. My mother's refusal to let me convert did real damage to the spiritual life of my family.

I wrote all of it down without a "but" and without explaining why they are the way they are.

**Defend the boundary.**

A boundary is something I do. Everything on my list is a request, and they can say no. My dad isn't taking my calls. My mom has dementia and may not remember what I say. So the list is the terms for repair, and my boundaries are what I do while those terms aren't met.

The Catechism says forgiveness isn't something we can feel on command, and that a heart given to the Holy Spirit turns injury into compassion (CCC 2843). I can release the debt and still enforce boundaries.

**Own my part.**

This is the hard one. With Missy, I put my parents ahead of her. When I was hurting, I went to work friends or games and didn't tell her what I felt. I wasn't curious about her pain. When she defended herself, I heard an attack.

With my daughter, I wasn't curious when she was hurting and I let her sit in it alone. On the day she needed me, I chose work.

I don't get to list what my parents owe me and skip this. Jesus says to take the log out of your own eye first (Matthew 7:5). 

I don't know if my father will ever say the sentence I need. I wrote it down anyway. It's short, and it's enough: he says it happened. If he never does, I'll still have done my part.

Lord, the door is open. I'm not standing in it. I'm doing the work inside.

*Search me, O God, and know my heart; test me and know my thoughts.*

**- Psalm 139:23**
