Lord,

I'm grateful to you. There was a time in the middle of all this when I didn't see a way out. I've kept showing up, and you've been with me. I've tried to listen.

You asked me to be present with my family and keep them top of mind. I've done that with some success. I'm trying to keep them in focus even as you've blessed me with success at work and in the foundation.

Three years ago I was looking for comparative metrics because I wasn't sure I was working on the right things. I had the trappings of success and something felt wrong. In your time, the truth came out as I invested more in you.

In the darkness I couldn't see the other end. I see it now. It involves work I know how to do: hard conversations, witnessing, trying to understand with an open heart, learning from failure, getting up and trying again.

One day I hope to be in a place to repair things with my parents. Repairing my immediate family comes first.

After Thanksgiving, Missy and I are going through the grief handbook. We're going to witness each other's pain and seek completion in you and in each other. I'm asking Abby to name the things keeping her from going back to school, and then we'll work on them together.

In parallel, you've given me the chance to help others through my work and the foundation. You have taken care of us. This semester started with 30 students and ended with 10, and the NPS was 0. I haven't heard from the 20 who left. My guess is that our mentors are still presenting content. They want to know they're helping kids succeed, but when a kid isn't invested yet, the content gets in the way. Presence comes before truth. Students tell me through surveys. I'm going to sit down 1:1 with mentors and ask them directly. I'm also building a mentor course on whole-heart listening, and on explaining content without using it. Mentors will certify once a year. I'm still working out the details. There will be another semester to learn from. I've gotten what I could out of this one.

I believe a family that loves each other, and people in work that fits their ikigai, is a world you want. People using their talents for you and for each other. In the end it's your will, not mine. Remind me, Lord, to pause and check for your fruits, so I know I'm listening to your signals and not my own rationalization.
