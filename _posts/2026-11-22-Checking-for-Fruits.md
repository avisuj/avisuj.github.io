Lord,

I'm grateful to you. There was a time in the middle of all this when I didn't see a way out. I've kept showing up, and you've been with me. I've tried to listen.

You've given me a lot. A good family. Gifts. People around me who help turn those gifts into impact. Somewhere in that abundance I can get lost and think it's me. It's you. I see it most clearly when I'm watching the wrong things, like how I'm performing next to other people.

Three years ago I was looking for comparative metrics because I wasn't sure I was working on the right things. I had the trappings of success and something felt wrong. In your time, the truth came out as I invested more in you.

In the parable of the talents, the servant with two hears the same words as the servant with five: "Well done, good and faithful servant" (Matthew 25:21, 23). Nobody in that story is measured against anyone else. The one who buries his talent does it out of fear (Matthew 25:25). I know both of those pulls.

In the darkness I couldn't see the other end. I see it now. It involves work I know how to do: hard conversations, witnessing, trying to understand with an open heart, learning from failure, getting up and trying again.

Baptism made me priest, prophet, and king (CCC 783). I'm trying to live that out where I am.

As a priest, I offer my family to you. After Thanksgiving, Missy and I are going through the grief handbook. We're going to witness each other's pain and seek completion in you and in each other. I'm asking Abby to name the things keeping her from going back to school, and then we'll work on them together.

As a prophet, I tell the truth, and presence has to come first. The foundation started this semester with 30 students and ended with 10. I haven't heard from the 20 who left. My guess is that our mentors are still presenting content, and when a kid isn't invested yet, the content gets in the way. I'm going to sit down 1:1 with mentors and ask them directly. I'm also building a mentor course on whole-heart listening, and on explaining content without using it.

As a king, I serve, because to reign is to serve (CCC 786). One day I hope to be in a place to repair things with my parents. Repairing my immediate family comes first. I want to make failure safe in my home and at the foundation, so nobody buries what you gave them out of fear.

I believe a family that loves each other, and people in work that fits their ikigai, is a world you want. People using their talents for you and for each other. In the end it's your will, not mine. Remind me, Lord, to pause and check for your fruits, so I know I'm listening to your signals and not my own rationalization.

Amen.

*But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law.*

**- Galatians 5:22-23**
