The Sujeeth Foundation kicked off with a coat of arms exercise. Four quadrants. One of them asked where I wanted to be in five years.

I wrote that I wanted to do things that made my heart warm. I wanted to travel more.

Then I looked at what I'd written. It was a wake-up call. I had let shame anchor me in my past. I had stopped being here for now, and I had stopped looking ahead.

Around then I wrote my own obituary. I wanted to die surrounded by my family. I want to be remembered as a Catholic father who stayed when it got hard. I want to be proud of the work in the Foundation. I want to travel the world with Missy.

All of it is still available to me.

To get there, I need to be present with my kids and help coach them as they find their way. I need to be present with my wife. I need to witness and comfort, and not judge or fix. I also need to treat this the way I treat work: know where I am, know where I want to go, and take the next step.

I've been reading Why Won't You Apologize? It has a story about a daughter who felt abandoned after counseling, because nobody ever talked about what had happened to her. I read it and thought of my own house. I need to be curious. I need to lead my child through something terrible.

I failed her. To fail is to be human. I failed because I carried shame, and I don't have to carry it. Lord, you died for me. I am heir to your legacy. Baptism made me priest, prophet, and king (CCC 783). I am not my worst day. I can be better.

Give me the strength to show up now. Help me ask the hard questions and listen with an open heart. Give her the strength to put one foot in front of the other. Let love knit our family back together through this. I know you're still working in us.

I'm about 25% of the way to forgiving my parents. I want to forgive them. I want to repair that relationship. It can't happen until we survive this season, and it can't happen at Missy's expense. It has to happen with her. Give me patience.

In five years my kids will be in college. Missy and I will be traveling. The Foundation will be moving forward. Maybe I'll be able to retire. It might take another ten years, or twenty. It doesn't matter, because I'll be living a life worth living.
