We moved to St. Louis this year because I wanted my family safer. It worked. Missy and the kids started laughing again. We started having hard conversations instead of avoiding them. All of us exhaled.

Because we were safe, I could finally look at what I'd been outrunning.

This isn't a resolved post. I don't have the ending yet. I'm writing it from inside the mess, three weeks into counseling, because that's the only honest place to write it from.

---

### Session One: The Calendar

I went into counseling carrying a penance from confession: forgive my parents. So I did what I do. I built a document. Thirty years, laid out plainly: situation, outcome, feeling. The kitchen fire. The caning. The gifted program. The threats to cut me off. The wedding my father skipped.

> *You have searched me, Lord, and you know me.*
> *You know when I sit and when I rise;*
> *you perceive my thoughts from afar.*
> — Psalm 139:1-2

He was there for all thirty years of it. I wasn't showing Him anything new. I was showing myself.

---

### Session Two: The Bus Stop

Second appointment, my counselor asked the question I came in expecting: could I forgive my parents. Except he wasn't asking about the calendar anymore. He was asking about Abby.

I said no.

My father was watching her. He didn't walk her to the bus stop. Something happened in that gap. When she came home late, he didn't ask what happened — he yelled at her. My parents have never acknowledged the pain they caused or contributed to. Given the choice between facing it and pretending we don't exist, they've chosen, over and over, to pretend.

When I actually sat with that, it hit like a fist. Stomach and throat, both at once. I wanted to rush in and hold her, shield her, be the wall between her and whatever was coming. I couldn't. Not that day — I wasn't there. Not now — it already happened, and holding her today doesn't undo it.

> *How long, Lord? Will you forget me forever?*
> *How long will you hide your face from me?*
> — Psalm 13:1

I didn't know yet I was about to ask God the same question I was asking my father.

---

### The Pivot: Hiding His Face

My counselor asked what was standing in the way of accepting what happened to Abby. I found the answer before I wanted to.

If I accept it, I have to accept God didn't intervene. That He let this evil happen to my daughter, to my family. That I trusted Him, and He hid His face.

And under that: maybe He doesn't exist. And if He does, maybe He doesn't want a relationship with me.

I've called my faith a choice, not a revelation — Pascal's Wager, do the math, land on belief. That's not the whole truth. I've felt God at church. I've felt Him in prayer. I felt Him clearly at a retreat when I was seventeen. Those were real.

So the wager isn't because I doubt the feelings happened. It's because a wager is safer than a relationship. You can lose a wager and just be wrong about the math. You can't lose a relationship that way — a relationship can betray you. I think part of me would rather bet than be betrayed again, by a Father who was supposed to be nothing like the one I already have.

This is fucking evil. What happened to my daughter is evil, and I'm not reaching for a softer word yet. I've pulled myself out of bad hands, with the help of good people along the way, but alone where it mattered most. So when I needed a Father to show up and got silence, it confirmed the oldest thing I know: nobody's coming. Not even Him.

> *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?*
> *Why are you so far from saving me,*
> *so far from my cries of anguish?*
> — Psalm 22:1

Christ prayed that from the cross. I don't think this doubt puts me outside the faith. 

---

### Session Three: Resignation or Celebration

Then my counselor asked what acceptance would look like. My first word was resignation. I was thinking about what happened to Abby, and resignation was the only shape I had for it — tolerating something unbearable.

But I don't think that's the right word. I think it's celebration. Not celebrating what happened to her. Celebrating who she is now. Saying that out loud, I noticed I've been struggling to hold both — the daughter I remember and the daughter in front of me.

The daughter I remember was happy. Curious. She laughed a lot. She was a cuddler. I miss that girl. I'm allowed to miss her.

This Abby has been tested. She's more resilient and I am proud of her. She's doing hard, real work right now, rebuilding something. She's not a lesser version of who she was. She's a different chapter of the same person, and she deserves to be seen fully in it — not measured against who she used to be.

My counselor asked what acceptance looks like in the other directions too. I'm still guessing at most of this, but here's where I landed:

**My father** — not excusing him. Recognizing his behavior was predictable, that he was never set up for success, and that he probably doesn't have the capacity to ever own what he's failed to own. It's sad. He's limited in a way that has nothing to do with how much I've wanted him to be more.

**My family** — recognizing the grief we're moving through together, and understanding the love is in the investment itself. Staying at the foundation. Doing the slow rebuild even when it hurts.

**Myself** — admitting I have limitations. That I have needs. That I'm doing the best I can, and my bones will break if I keep pretending otherwise. Success was never supposed to mean raising a traditionally successful child. It means loving them, and helping them become who they actually want to be.

> *The Lord is close to the brokenhearted*
> *and saves those who are crushed in spirit.*
> — Psalm 34:18

I don't feel that closeness most days right now. But I know where to find the verse. Some nights that's the whole of what I've got — not the feeling, just the address.

---

### A Prayer

God, I don't know if You're there. I've said that out loud now, to a counselor and to myself and now to whoever reads this. 

I am angry at You. Not in the abstract. Angry that Abby was hurt while my father looked away, and angrier that You looked away too, if You were even there to look. I don't want a lecture about free will. I don't want the meaning yet, if there's meaning to be had. I want You to know I'm furious.

I don't know if my faith is a revelation or a choice I keep making because the alternative is worse. Maybe today it's just the choice. I'm making it anyway.

Help me hold Abby exactly as she is, not as I remember her, without asking her to be less wounded than she is so I can feel less helpless.

Help me see my father clearly — small enough to stop expecting what he cannot give, without letting him off the hook for what he owes.

Help me believe, if You're willing to help with that too, that You have not hidden Your face. That You are close to the brokenhearted even when I can't feel it. That the silence isn't proof You're gone. It might just be proof I'm finally quiet enough to notice it.

I don't have an ending for this. I'm not going to force one.

Amen. Fuck.
